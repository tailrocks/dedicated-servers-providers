# Which CI machine to buy

**Decision as of 20 November 2026, Asia/Saigon.** If the purchase must replace one AX162's parallel build capacity and keep ordinary non-ECC RAM, select the **Singapore 5995WX / WRX80, 256 GB (8×32 GB) and 2×2 TB Lexar NM790 at S$13,330**. The 2×4 TB version is **S$14,040** and covers both AX162 capacity dimensions at once. Buy these workstation parts as a coordinated order because CPU allocation, the listed cooler and warranty coverage for the legacy board are uncertain. The cheaper way to keep one similarly capable machine can be to rent the same 9454P. For a budget purchase with flexible queue scheduling, start with the **S$4,757 Intel 128 GB desktop**. For independent parallel jobs with a throughput target above the AX162, the **S$10,228 pair of 9950X3D desktops** is the better-supported owned pool. Measured rate percentages refer to the short Linux Geekbench 6 Clang subtest, unless Chromium is named. The candidate workstations and submissions had different boards, memory and operating conditions. Two runs on another 9454P system move the same-version comparison to **98.6% or 106.2%**. This evidence supports choosing the closest available system now, with **moderate confidence on the short CPU proxy and low confidence of a ±3–5% match for the complete Rust, TypeScript and Docker pipeline**. A 125.4% parallel Clang result exists for Threadripper 9970X, but the documented TRX50 board requires ECC RDIMMs. [Benchmarks and denominators](published-benchmarks.md) · [CPU alternatives](cpu-alternatives.md) · [CI interpretation](ci-cd-performance-analysis.md)

For C, each Ryzen returned 78.1–79.8% of the named AX162's parallel Clang rate. Two simultaneously busy nodes imply 156.2–159.6% before coordination, cache duplication and idle periods. At 80% efficiency the result is 125.0–127.7%; at 90% it is 140.6–143.7%. These are assumed queue scenarios. A serial dependency chain or single large linker cannot combine the machines' CPU or RAM. A second host adds another 2 TB mirror in a different filesystem. Two Intel 128 GB workers cost S$9,514 and suit the same workload shape, although the variation in Intel's public parallel measurements makes the throughput projection less certain. The SG WRX80 base is **S$3,102 more** than the complete two-node Ryzen pool, but it can run a single memory-heavy job above 128 GB and has eight memory channels, independent remote management and dual 10 GbE. The Ryzen pool provides two separately schedulable machines with faster lightly threaded stages. 128 GB DDR5 Intel with two 4 TB drives is a strong choice when per-job memory and disk capacity matter more than matching the target's parallel compile rate. A single 256 GB WRX80 remains the closest candidate when job size or compiler concurrency rules out that trade. The preferred NVMe is the two-drive Lexar NM790 Gen4 pair. The 2 TB pair costs S$1,008 or RM2,860. Singapore's 4 TB pair costs S$1,718. Use the drives as a mirror when local availability matters. Two separate scratch/cache volumes yield more raw capacity but different failure and restore behavior. The exact Hetzner DC SSD, firmware, PLP and latency profile remain unspecified. The comparison of retail TLC, endurance and independent sustained-write tests is in the [NVMe analysis](nvme-comparison.md). The [Hetzner baseline](hetzner-ax162-baseline.md) documents the differences in capacity, networking and DC storage.

The parts totals cover coolers, fans, case and PSU as well as CPU, motherboard, RAM and SSDs. Graphics, wired Ethernet, mounting and ordinary retail power cables are supplied by the chosen components. Existing site network and patch lead are assumed. Delivery where unquoted, optional assembly and a UPS would add to these part subtotals. Complete cost comparisons require the applicable local IT load, electricity and incremental internet service.
