# Which non-ECC CPU platform can replace an EPYC 9454P?

**Research date 20 November 2026, Asia/Saigon.** For one physical runner, choose **Threadripper PRO 5995WX, ASUS Pro WS WRX80E-SAGE SE WIFI and eight 32 GB non-ECC DDR4 UDIMMs** as the closest supported purchase candidate. It has 64 cores, eight populated channels and 256 GB available to one build. In a published parallel Clang subtest it reached **103.0% of a named Hetzner AX162-R**. An independent Dell 5995WX paired with two same-version Dell 9454P runs gives **98.6% or 106.2%**. The exact mixed CI result remains unmeasured. An existing AX162 is the most defensible choice if 95–105% performance is contractual and one host is required.

**For a mainstream desktop**, the Ryzen 9 9950X3D is the best balance of single-job speed and Singapore price. The Core Ultra 7 270K Plus is the lowest-cost matched node here. The 9950X3D2 has the shortest published general desktop compilation times and delivers more modeled compilation throughput per ringgit than X3D in the priced Malaysian 128 GB configuration. **For a busy queue of independent jobs**, select two 9950X3D hosts for the higher-throughput Singapore AMD option, or two 270K Plus hosts for the lower initial cost. Two mainstream hosts can plausibly reach the 110–150% aggregate target under the assumptions below. No eligible purchase is proven to deliver 110–150% for one ordinary unsharded pipeline.

### Why WRX80 qualifies, and which workstation platforms fail

The original **ASUS Pro WS WRX80E-SAGE SE WIFI** explicitly supports non-ECC unbuffered DDR4. ASUS lists the 5995WX on this board from BIOS 1003 and shows the 32 GB Crucial CT32G4DFD832A.C16FE at 3200 MT/s with eight sockets populated. This establishes a **256 GB non-ECC arrangement**, while the numerous registered-memory configurations on the same board explain its larger 2 TB product maximum. Exact module revision and board are material to sourcing. A different OEM tower may have a more restrictive firmware or memory list. [ASUS board data sheet](https://dlcdnets.asus.com/pub/ASUS/mb/SocketTRX4/Pro_WS_WRX80E-SAGE_SE_WIFI/PRO_WS_WRX80E-SAGE_SE_WIFI_SPEC.pdf) · [ASUS CPU support](https://www.asus.com/motherboards-components/motherboards/workstation/pro-ws-wrx80e-sage-se-wifi/helpdesk_cpu?model2Name=Pro-WS-WRX80E-SAGE-SE-WIFI) · [ASUS memory QVL](https://www.asus.com/us/motherboards-components/motherboards/workstation/pro-ws-wrx80e-sage-se-wifi/helpdesk_qvl_memory?model2Name=Pro-WS-WRX80E-SAGE-SE-WIFI) · [AMD 5000 WX lanes and rated memory](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-3-8-new-amd-ryzen-threadripper-pro-5000-wx-series-proc.html)

The ASUS-tested Crucial revision underlies the separate refurbished price scenario. [Module-specific evidence](ram-comparison.md) · [Local bill](recommended-builds.md)

### Memory topology and storage expansion

**Derived peak payload bandwidth** is active channels × 8 data bytes × transfers per second. This gives **460.8 GB/s** for 12 populated DDR5-4800 EPYC channels, **204.8 GB/s** for eight DDR4-3200 WRX80 channels, **102.4 GB/s** for four DDR4-3200 TRX40 channels and **89.6 GB/s** for dual DDR5-5600 AM5. One 128 GB 9454P submission shows 4×32 GB. If those four DIMMs occupy separate channels its ceiling is 153.6 GB/s. Hetzner's listed capacities alone do not establish channel count or frequency. The 270K Plus reaches a theoretical 115.2 GB/s only at dual-channel DDR5-7200. In practice capacity, DIMM rank, BIOS support and sustained access patterns determine bandwidth. [AMD memory population guide](https://www.amd.com/content/dam/amd/en/documents/epyc-technical-docs/user-guides/amd-epyc-9004-ug-memory-population-recommendations.pdf) · [Four-DIMM server observation](https://openbenchmarking.org/result/2507076-NE-AMDEPYC9444)

For 9950X-family desktops AMD specifies DDR5-5600 with two DIMMs and DDR5-3600 with four. At four modules dual-channel DDR5-3600 has a 57.6 GB/s theoretical ceiling at the rated speed. A higher EXPO/XMP rating describes an overclock profile. A 2×64 GB quote avoids the four-DIMM penalty at 128 GB. WRX80 can connect two local NVMe drives and a high-speed NIC without the limited lane budget of a desktop, although each board fixes which slots share resources. Two mainstream runners provide two sets of local drives and two separate filesystem caches. [RAM compatibility and prices](ram-comparison.md) · [NVMe selection](nvme-comparison.md)
