# Published build benchmarks against the EPYC 9454P

**Checked 20 November 2026, Asia/Saigon.** The strongest public buying case for one non-ECC host is the 64-core Threadripper PRO 5995WX on WRX80. Two different workstations achieved 333.8 and 337.1 thousand lines per second in a short parallel Clang compile. A named Hetzner AX162-R achieved 327.4. A long Chromium build on the 5995WX also has published results, although that review used a different comparison CPU. No identified publication timed the same Rust, TypeScript and Docker CI pipeline on an AX162 and a candidate workstation.

## How percentages are calculated

**Direct** means an original measurement or published configuration. **Derived** means arithmetic using identified observations. **Analytical** means a conditional scenario. **Unavailable** means the required pair of measurements or operating condition could not be established. Independent public submissions are direct observations of those machines. A ratio across submissions is a derived comparison with differing configurations.

For a work rate, the candidate index is `100 × candidate rate / reference rate`. For elapsed seconds on the same work it is `100 × reference seconds / candidate seconds`. A 125% rate means 80% of the reference time for the same fixed work. For the buying question the 95–105% rate band corresponds to about 95.2–105.3% of reference elapsed time. The faster band of 110–150% corresponds to 90.9–66.7% of reference time. All indexes below name the workload and denominator. They do not form a cross-workload average.

### How sensitive is the 5995WX conclusion to the reference?

Two **6.5.0** Debian 12 runs from the same account on a Dell R6615 with a 9454P, uploaded 74 minutes apart, reported **10.9/338.7** and **10.9/314.3**. The Dell Precision 5995WX at **10.9/333.8** therefore comes out at **100% on single-core and 98.6% or 106.2% on multicore**. The two server submissions share the model, board and RAM and may be from the same physical machine. Their reported frequency fields are 2.75 and 3.81 GHz, respectively. The reason for the 7.8% difference between the two server work rates is unavailable. Another named AX162 with a 9454P, Ubuntu 20.04.6 and 188.39 GB returned **10.5/297.1** on 6.3.0. Capacity, RAM layout, thermals, software and power policy can change the denominator. [Dell run at 11:10](https://browser.geekbench.com/v6/cpu/15028414) · [Dell run at 12:24](https://browser.geekbench.com/v6/cpu/15029468) · [Independent AX162](https://browser.geekbench.com/v6/cpu/10492158)

The same-version observations of 98.6%, 103.0% and 106.2% establish the scale of the compiler comparison. They do not establish a confidence interval. The 6.4.0 WRX80 workstation reports a frequency field of 4.57 GHz against a 4.5 GHz advertised maximum. The separate 6.5 workstation reports 4.58 GHz. Those metadata are insufficient to establish sustained stock behavior. A further Gigabyte 5995WX upload reports 7.02 GHz and 15.41 GB, so it is excluded from the main comparison. Public benchmark pages do not confirm that the tested memory matches the proposed non-ECC DIMMs. [Excluded submission](https://browser.geekbench.com/v6/cpu/15938535) · [AMD 5995WX specifications](https://www.amd.com/en/support/downloads/drivers.html/processors/ryzen-threadripper-pro/ryzen-threadripper-pro-5000wx-series/amd-ryzen-threadripper-pro-5995wx.html)

### Rust compilation and TypeScript checking

**Direct.** OpenBenchmarking `pts/build-wasmer-1.3.0` compiles the Rust project Wasmer 7.0.1. The November 16 aggregate covers 383 submissions across all hardware. These component averages combine differing environments. Counts in this table are compatible public results and can include repeated submissions. The host does not specify a statistical interpretation for the displayed ± quantities used here. [Original profile and results](https://mail.openbenchmarking.org/test/pts/build-wasmer)

| CPU | Compatible results | Reported compile time, seconds |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ryzen 9 9950X3D2 | 13 | 76 ± 9 |
| Ryzen 9 9950X3D | 6 | 78 ± 11 |
| Threadripper 9980X | 6 | 78 ± 1 |
| Core Ultra 7 270K Plus | 10 | 82 ± 2 |
| Threadripper 3990X | 4 | 83 |
| Ryzen 9 9950X | 4 | 85 |
| Core Ultra 9 285K | 3 | 87 |
| EPYC 9454P / Threadripper PRO 5995WX | No component row located for this profile | Unavailable |

The 16-core X3D and 64-core 9980X both averaged 78 s on this Rust project. Other tests favor the 64-core processor by much more. Neither that equality nor the difference between 76 s and 78 s resolves a customer's clean cargo workspace, incremental check, test execution or builds/hour.

**Direct software comparison.** Microsoft reports **125.7 s vs 10.6 s** to build VS Code on TypeScript 6 vs 7, with **5.2 GB vs 4.2 GB** of memory, and **12.8 s vs 1.47 s** for Playwright. TypeScript 7 uses shared-memory parallelism and its default four type-checking workers. With eight workers on the same VS Code project, the reported time was 7.51 s. These results compare compiler versions and settings, with no AX162/HEDT CPU comparison. They illustrate why the lockfile and worker counts change the hardware conclusion. [Microsoft's measurement and methodology](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/typescript/announcing-typescript-7-0/)

### Supporting throughput and power data

* **Direct, synthetic.** PassMark's same comparison page showed CPU Mark **94,689 vs 94,796** and single-thread **2,988 vs 3,216** for 9454P and 5995WX, with 126 and 190 samples. The 5995WX indexes are **100.1%** aggregate and **107.6%** single-thread. The aggregate corroborates scale, while the gap from the single-core Clang ranking illustrates workload dependence. [PassMark comparison](https://www.cpubenchmark.net/compare/5596vs4764/AMD-EPYC-9454P-vs-AMD-Ryzen-Threadripper-PRO-5995WX)
* **Direct, server baseline.** Lenovo's single-socket 9454P scored **525 SPECrate2017_int_base** with 12×32 GB DDR5 and 96 rate copies. Its selected `502.gcc_r` base result was 314 seconds with a ratio of 433. This runs many copies of a compiler workload and uses a fully populated server. A matched published desktop SPEC result was not located. [SPEC submission](https://www.spec.org/cpu2017/results/res2023q1/cpu2017-20230227-34459.html)
* **Direct, different power boundaries.** Phoronix's Linux suite reports average CPU power of **184 W X3D2**, **165 W X3D**, **164 W 270K Plus** and an X3D2 peak of 261 W. PC Perspective's complete P620/5995WX workstation drew about **435 W at the wall** under Blender CPU load and peaked at **461 W**. These workloads and measurement boundaries cannot establish continuous office CI electricity or directly comparable joules/build. [Phoronix CPU monitoring](https://www.phoronix.com/review/amd-ryzen-9950x3d2-linux/11) · [PC Perspective wall measurement](https://pcper.com/2023/01/lenovo-thinkstation-p620-review-ryzen-threadripper-pro-5995wx-beast/)

**Unavailable.** No controlled production-pipeline comparison was found for the specified CPUs, cache states and runner concurrency. In particular there is no supported CI percentage for Docker build layers, TypeScript tests, a full Rust build/test pipeline, or the 1/4/8/16/32/64-job queue. The separate [concurrency analysis](ci-cd-performance-analysis.md) shows what can be inferred from published rates, memory and parallelism.
